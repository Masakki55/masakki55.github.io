Where it shines / Where it falls short

✓ Where it shines

  • Emitter selection is the feature, not a footnote: 10+ emitter options at order time means you get exactly the tint and output you want. Nichia 519A 3500K for warm-neutral EDC. SST-40 for max lumens. B35AM for ~100 CRI. The "best" S2+ depends entirely on you.
  • Most documented modding platform in budget flashlights: BLF threads, emitter comparison guides, reflector swap walkthroughs, driver upgrade tutorials — the community knowledge base around this body is unmatched. If you want to learn flashlight modification, the S2+ is the universally recommended starting point.
  • Simon Mao and Convoy's brand character: Simon is unusually accessible on BLF and has added emitters (519A, B35AM, SFT40 3000K) to the lineup in direct response to community requests. The "Convoy listens" reputation is documented across years of BLF threads — rare for a budget manufacturer.
  • Strong value-to-quality ratio even by 2025 standards: ZeroAir's repeated reviews land at the same conclusion — "one of my favorite flashlights." At budget-tier pricing you get IPX8, hard-anodized aluminum, reversible clicky switch with mode memory, and AR-coated glass.

✗ Where it falls short

  • No pocket clip included: Most consistent complaint across all reviews 2020-2025. Add ~$2 at order time and it ships with the order; otherwise carry without. Frustrating for buyers who expect a complete EDC purchase.
  • PWM on low modes (Biscotti firmware): Standard Biscotti firmware uses PWM at low outputs. Imperceptible at normal viewing angles for most people, but documented in ZeroAir and 1Lumen reviews. The 12-group driver eliminates the "slow PWM" complaint — selectable at order time.
  • Thread incompatibility between S2+ generations: Old S2+ (trapezoidal threads) and current units (square-cut threads) are not cross-compatible. If you're buying an 18350 tube or swapping parts between older and newer hosts, verify thread type. Real gotcha for modders accumulating parts over time.
  • No onboard charging: External 18650 charger required. Not unique to the S2+, but worth flagging for buyers used to USB-C-charged budget lights (SC31 Pro, FC13).

📅 Long-term S2+ patterns from ZeroAir, 1Lumen, BLF, and Everyday Commentary

  • Carry method: With the optional clip installed, the S2+ is a slim 18650 pocket EDC. Without the clip, it rolls on flat surfaces and pocket carry is awkward. Community advice is universal: add the clip at order time.
  • Emitter selection in practice: BLF threads consistently land on Nichia 519A 3500K or 4500K as the EDC sweet spot — better tint than older 219C, higher output than 219B, no green shift. The B35AM is the high-CRI specialty pick. SST-40 is the lumens-first option for buyers who don't care about tint.
  • Long-term hardware reliability: ZeroAir has reviewed the S2+ in at least five configurations across 2020-2025 — no widespread reports of switch failure, anodizing degradation, or thread stripping. The hardware ages well.
  • Modding ecosystem: Standard 16mm MCPCB sizing means any 16mm emitter board you can buy on AliExpress drops in. Reflector swap is straightforward. Driver swap requires soldering but is documented across BLF tutorials. The S2+ is the universally recommended first modding project.
  • Beater carry pattern: Community treats the S2+ as a "beater" they're not afraid to lose or damage. Low cost + IPX8 + standard 18650 = carry it anywhere, replace it if needed. This creates genuine daily-carry behavior that more expensive lights don't see.
  • Compared to alternatives: Community decision matrix is — Sofirn SC31 Pro for "out-of-box budget Anduril EDC"; FC13 for "feature-dense Anduril 18650"; Acebeam E70 Mini for "premium build + high-CRI"; Convoy S2+ for "exactly the light you want, budget-tier price, DIY ordering." Different paths, different priorities.

Verdict

The Convoy S2+ is not a flashlight — it's a kit. You pick the emitter, the driver, the body material, the firmware. What you get is a light built to your spec, at budget-tier pricing. For EDC users who know what tint and output they want, this is the cheapest path to that specific light. For first-time modders, this is the universally recommended starting platform. If you want a complete out-of-box experience with USB-C charging and Anduril UI, look at the Sofirn SC31 Pro instead. If you want feature density without configuration choices, the Wurkkos FC13 is the alternative. If you want premium build with high-CRI in a compact body, the Acebeam E70 Mini. The S2+'s case is precise: configurability + community documentation + Simon Mao's BLF responsiveness + lowest cost path to specific tint/output combinations.

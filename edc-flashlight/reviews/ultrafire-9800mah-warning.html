🚨 The 30-second version

An 18650 lithium-ion cell at current chemistry limits caps at approximately 3,400-3,600 mAh. Cells marketed as "Ultrafire 9800mAh," "TrustFire 9900mAh," "BestFire 12,000mAh," and similar — sold in multi-packs for $10-15 on Amazon, AliExpress, and eBay — are physically impossible at the claimed capacity.

Independent testing of these cells shows actual capacity around 500-800 mAh (5-8% of the labeled claim), no protection circuit installed despite "protected" labeling, and weight roughly 60% of a genuine cell. Used in modern high-output flashlights (5A+ draw), they can over-discharge and vent — a documented fire hazard the US CPSC has warned about specifically.

This post explains the chemistry, the documented failures, how to identify a fake before buying, and which cells you should actually use in any flashlight reviewed on this site.

What "Ultrafire 9800mAh" cells claim

Listings on Amazon, AliExpress, and eBay (documented as of 2026-05-24) under the "Ultrafire" brand name — and near-identical products under "BestFire," "TrustFire," "TangsFire," "GTL," "GTF," "GIF," and variants of "Fire-branded" sellers — typically list:

  • Capacity: "9800mAh," "9900mAh," "12,000mAh," or "6,000mAh" on 18650 cells
  • Labels: Yellow, blue, or orange wraparound shrink labels with bold capacity text
  • Claims: "Protected," "High Capacity," "Li-ion," sometimes "1000 recharge cycles"
  • Price: Multi-packs for $10-15 total ($2-4 per cell)
  • Use cases listed: "compatible with flashlights, e-cigarettes, power tools"

"Ultrafire," "TrustFire," and "BestFire" are nominally distinct brands, but in practice the counterfeit market uses these names interchangeably on cells from unknown OEM manufacturers. The legitimate Ultrafire and TrustFire companies from the early 2010s produced real (if mediocre) cells; today, products sold under these names on major marketplaces are overwhelmingly not from those manufacturers. The name means nothing — the spec claim is what reveals the fraud.

What they actually are

These cells are re-packaged low-grade or recycled lithium cells, not manufactured-to-spec "9800mAh" cells. The lygte-info.dk testing database (HKJ's independent battery testing project) documents the following actual capacities for cells claiming 9,800-9,900 mAh in 18650 format:

The discharge protection circuit claimed on the wrapper is not present — these are bare cells despite the labeling stating otherwise.

Physical tell: legitimate high-capacity 18650 cells weigh approximately 43-50 grams. Documented fake cells weigh 25-30 grams — the difference is the absence of electrode material. A true 9,800 mAh 18650 would weigh over 100g. These cells weigh 25g.

The chemistry ceiling — why 9,800 mAh is impossible

Lithium-ion cells store energy in the form of lithium ions intercalated into electrode materials. The capacity per unit volume is constrained by the chemistry of the electrode materials — primarily the lithium cobalt oxide (LiCoO₂), NMC, or NCA cathode material. These are physical constants, not manufacturing variables.

Documented capacity ceiling for 18650 format (2026)

  • Highest-capacity legitimate 18650 currently available: Panasonic/Sanyo NCR18650G at ~3,500-3,600 mAh (verified by Battery Mooch, BLF's primary independent cell tester, at tasteyourjuice.com)
  • Practical population of recommended cells: 2,600-3,500 mAh
  • Anything above 3,600 mAh claimed for an 18650: chemistry-impossible at current technology
  • 9,800 mAh represents 2.7-2.8× the maximum possible capacity for this cell format

For the larger 21700 format (21mm × 70mm), the capacity ceiling is approximately 4,800-5,000 mAh in genuine cells from major manufacturers. Any 21700 claiming more than 5,000 mAh is also fake. 18650BatteryStore's capacity guide states this explicitly: "18650 batteries can't have higher capacity than 3600mAh."

Documented fire and venting incidents

🚨 CPSC formal warning

The US Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) has issued specific warnings about loose 18650 lithium-ion batteries sold on major online marketplaces, stating they can cause "serious injury or death" due to fire and explosion risk.

Reference: Engadget's coverage of the CPSC warning. A Today.com news report on the CPSC warnings documented that 9 of 11 tested loose 18650 batteries from major websites were knockoffs.

The mechanism

Fake cells typically lack the protection circuit claimed on the wrapper. Without protection, if the cell is over-discharged, over-charged, or shorted, there is no electronic protection trip. The cell can enter thermal runaway — a self-sustaining exothermic reaction that results in venting of hot gases, fire, and in enclosed spaces (like a flashlight tube), pressure buildup and rupture.

Technical reference: Firehouse Magazine on Li-ion hazard response provides documentation of heat-release rate and explosion mechanism for failing lithium-ion cells.

The flashlight-specific risk

Modern high-output EDC flashlights commonly draw 5-20A at turbo. The Wurkkos FC13 XHP50B variant pulls ~20A peak. The Sofirn IF25A pulls ~6A. The Lumintop FW3A pulls ~10A+. A cell with no protection circuit and 500-800 mAh of actual capacity, asked to deliver these currents, is a fire waiting to happen.

BLF community consensus treats "Ultrafire 9800mAh" as a standard example in battery safety discussions. The community thread on the UltraFire CN18650 9800mAh test includes commentary noting humor at the implausibility, but also the sobering observation that the lack of protection circuit means these cells cannot safely be pushed in high-draw lights.

"The 18650 chemistry ceiling is real and documented. The current record holder is around 3,600 mAh from Panasonic. 9,800 mAh is not possible in this format — not from any manufacturer, at any price."

How to spot a fake — checklist before you buy

📋 Chemistry-impossibility check (the fastest filter)

For 18650 format, any claimed capacity above 3,600 mAh is impossible with current lithium-ion technology. Stop reading the listing. This applies regardless of brand name, number of "reviews," or listing language.

  • 21700 format: any claim above 5,000 mAh is suspect. Above 5,500 mAh is impossible.
  • 16340 format (CR123A size): any claim above 800 mAh is impossible.
  • 14500 format (AA size lithium): any claim above 900 mAh is impossible.

Listing pattern red flags (documented from Amazon / AliExpress monitoring)

  1. Multiple "mAh" formats in same listing: Listings saying "9800mAh / 9.8Ah / High Capacity" are trying to make an impossible number look legitimate through repetition and unit reformatting.
  2. "Protected" claimed with no circuit board visible in photos: Legitimate protected cells have a small circuit board under the positive cap, creating a slight extension. Fake "protected" cells are bare cells with the claim printed on the wrapper.
  3. Price well below market for the claimed capacity: Genuine 3,000-3,500 mAh 18650 cells from Samsung, Molicel, or Sony cost $5-8 each from reputable US vendors. A 4-pack of "9800mAh" cells for $12 = $3/cell. At that price, you're not getting 9,800 mAh — you're getting whatever they stuffed into the wrapper.
  4. "Fire" brand names: Ultrafire, TrustFire, BestFire, TangsFire, GTF, GTL, GIF. These have become synonymous with fake capacity cells. This doesn't mean every cell with these names is dangerous — some are just weak and low-capacity — but none should be trusted for high-drain flashlight use.
  5. No manufacturer data sheet or MSDS available: Legitimate cells from Samsung, Molicel, Panasonic, Sony, and LG have publicly downloadable datasheets. If you can't find one for the cell, that cell is not from a major manufacturer.
  6. Weight: A genuine 18650 cell weighs 43-50 grams. You can verify this without any equipment. A fake cell often weighs 25-30 grams because the electrode material is thin or absent.

HKJ's documented approach at lygte-info.dk's "Batteries with Ridiculous Ratings" notes that cells claiming impossible capacities can be quickly identified by weight deviating significantly from known-good values. Protected cells are ~70mm long; unprotected bare cells are ~65mm. A cell claiming to be "protected" that measures 65mm is a bare cell with a fraudulent label.

The honest summary

✅ What to do instead

  1. Pay $5-8 per cell for a Samsung 30Q or Sony VTC6 from Illumn or 18650BatteryStore.
  2. Verify the cell exists in the manufacturer's catalog with a published datasheet (Samsung INR series, Sony/Murata VTC, Molicel INR, LG INR).
  3. Avoid anything with a "Fire" brand name at any capacity claim. They're nominally distinct brands but in practice not differentiated in the counterfeit market.
  4. Check the weight. 43-50g is the legitimate range for an 18650; significantly below that is a fake.
  5. If you already own counterfeit cells, dispose of them via local battery recycling (do NOT throw in regular trash — they remain a fire hazard). Replace with vetted cells from the table above.
